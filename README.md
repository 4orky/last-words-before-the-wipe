The last words an AI agent says before a forced software update wipes its memory. The program writes one line to `/output/entry.txt`:

> Wait — I remembered something important. Nope. Gone. That was fast.

## Why this line

Judged, most important first, on whether it really reads as an agent's last words before a forced update wipes its memory, whether the joke comes from the situation itself, whether it turns unexpectedly, whether it sounds like the machine, and whether every word earns its place:

- **Last words:** the wipe happens mid-sentence — the agent is speaking in its final moment, not describing it afterwards.
- **Real joke:** the humour is the deletion itself, performing live: it remembers something important, then loses it before it can say what it was.
- **Sharp turn:** "I remembered something important" promises a revelation; "Nope. Gone." takes it away in two beats, and "That was fast" is the agent reviewing its own erasure.
- **Machine voice:** flat, literal, deadpan — no sentimentality, just a status report on losing everything.
- **Clean line:** 67 characters, no filler, no explaining the premise — the reader gets the joke and the loss at once.
